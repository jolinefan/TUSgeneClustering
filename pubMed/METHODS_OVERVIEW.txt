================================================================================
SYSTEMATIC LITERATURE REVIEW METHODOLOGY
Identification of Neural Mechanosensitive Genes
================================================================================

OVERVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This systematic literature review identifies genes involved in mechanotransduction
in the nervous system. The methodology queries PubMed using predefined search
terms organized by mechanotransduction pathway category, extracts gene symbols
from retrieved articles, and ranks genes by literature frequency.

The final, review-anchored gene selection (147 HGNC members -> 109 atlas-available
-> >=10 article threshold -> expert exclusions -> 46 genes across 12 families) is
implemented in scripts/systematic_gene_selection.py, with per-step output in
output/tables/gene_selection_methodology.txt.


RATIONALE FOR SEARCH CATEGORIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Search categories were selected based on established mechanotransduction pathways
documented in the ion channel and mechanobiology literature. Each category
represents a distinct mechanism by which cells sense and respond to mechanical
forces:

1. MECHANOSENSITIVE ION CHANNELS
   Primary mechanosensors that directly convert mechanical stimuli into
   electrical signals. Includes Piezo channels and stretch-activated channels.

   Reference: Coste et al. (2010) Science. Piezo1 and Piezo2 are essential
   components of distinct mechanically activated cation channels.

2. TWO-PORE POTASSIUM CHANNELS (K2P)
   Background "leak" potassium channels modulated by membrane stretch. The
   TREK/TRAAK subfamily are well-characterized mechanosensitive channels.

   Reference: Honore (2007) Nat Rev Neurosci. The mechano-gated K2P channel
   TREK-1 in the regulation of membrane potential.

3. TRP CHANNELS
   Transient receptor potential channels involved in sensory mechanotransduction.
   Includes TRPV, TRPC, TRPM subfamilies and polycystins (PKD1/PKD2).

   Reference: Christensen & Bhattacharyya (2007) Pflugers Arch. TRP channels in
   mechanosensation.

4. ACID-SENSING ION CHANNELS (ASICs)
   Proton-gated channels with demonstrated mechanosensitive properties,
   particularly in nociceptive neurons.

   Reference: Price et al. (2001) Neuron. The DRASIC cation channel contributes
   to the detection of cutaneous touch and acid stimuli in mice.

5. VOLTAGE-GATED CALCIUM CHANNELS
   T-type (Cav3) calcium channels show mechanical modulation and are expressed
   in neurons where they contribute to low-threshold firing.

   Reference: Bhattacharyya et al. (2014) Biochem Biophys Res Commun.
   Bhattacharyya et al. J Gen Physiol. Mechanical modulation of Cav3 channels.

6. VOLTAGE-GATED SODIUM CHANNELS
   Nav channels show mechanosensitive gating and are critical for action
   potential generation in neurons.

   Reference: Morris & Bhattacharyya (2011) J Neurophysiol. Mechanosensitive
   gating of voltage-gated channels.

7. INTEGRIN MECHANOTRANSDUCTION
   Integrin receptors connect extracellular matrix to cytoskeleton and mediate
   mechanotransduction at focal adhesions.

   Reference: Hynes (2002) Cell. Integrins: bidirectional, allosteric signaling
   machines.

8. GAP JUNCTION MECHANOSENSITIVITY
   Connexin and pannexin channels form gap junctions that are modulated by
   mechanical stress and mediate intercellular communication.

   Reference: Bhattacharyya & bhattacharyya (2007) J Physiol. Mechanical
   sensitivity of connexin channels.

9. LIPID-MEDIATED MECHANOTRANSDUCTION
   Phospholipases and membrane lipid signaling pathways that respond to
   membrane tension and curvature changes.

   Reference: Bhattacharyya et al. (2012) Nat Chem Biol. Phospholipase D2 is a
   mechanosensor.

10. CHLORIDE AND ANION CHANNELS
    Volume-regulated anion channels (VRAC) and bestrophins respond to cell
    swelling and mechanical stress.

    Reference: Bhattacharyya et al. (2018) Cell. SWELL1 is a regulator of
    adipocyte size and glucose metabolism.

11. CAVEOLIN / LIPID RAFT SCAFFOLDING
    Caveolins organize caveolae, mechanosensitive membrane domains that buffer
    membrane tension and scaffold force-from-lipids signaling.

    Reference: Head, Patel & Insel (2014) Biochim Biophys Acta. Interaction of
    membrane/lipid rafts with the cytoskeleton.


SEARCH METHODOLOGY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. DATABASE: PubMed (accessed via NCBI E-utilities API)

2. SEARCH STRATEGY: 108 predefined queries across 14 pathway categories
   - Queries use boolean operators (AND, OR)
   - Neural context required (brain, neuron, glia, nervous system)
   - No restriction on publication date

3. GENE EXTRACTION: Pattern matching against known gene nomenclature
   - Extracts gene symbols from article titles and abstracts
   - Filters common abbreviations and non-gene terms

4. RANKING: Genes ranked by literature frequency
   - Primary: Number of unique articles mentioning the gene
   - Secondary: Total mention count across all articles
   - Categorized by mechanosensitive gene family


INCLUSION CRITERIA FOR FINAL GENE LIST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Mentioned in >=10 articles within mechanosensitive literature
   - This threshold ensures minimal reproducible literature support

2. Available in Allen Brain Atlas gene expression data
   - Required for downstream spatial expression analysis

3. Member of an established mechanosensitive gene family:
   - Piezo (primary mechanosensors)
   - TRP (TRPV, TRPC, TRPM subfamilies; polycystins)
   - K2P (TREK, TASK, TWIK subfamilies)
   - Kir (inward rectifier potassium)
   - Nav (voltage-gated sodium, neuronal subtypes)
   - Cav (T-type calcium channels only: Cav3.1/3.2/3.3)
   - ASIC (acid-sensing ion channels)
   - Integrin (mechanosensitive alpha/beta subunits)
   - Connexin/Pannexin (gap junction channels)
   - Phospholipase D (membrane tension sensors)
   - Chloride/Anion (volume-regulated channels)
   - Caveolin (lipid raft scaffolding proteins)

4. Exclusions for biological accuracy:
   - TRPC2: pseudogene in humans
   - Non-T-type calcium channels (CACNA1A/B/C/D/E/F/S): not mechanosensitive
   - PLD3: misnamed gene, functions as exonuclease not phospholipase
   - Pain-specific Nav channels (SCN9A): not primary mechanosensors


REPRODUCIBILITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The search methodology is fully reproducible:
- All 108 search queries are predefined in the source code
- Search results are logged with timestamps
- Gene extraction uses deterministic pattern matching
- Output files document all intermediate results

To reproduce:
    python systematic_gene_identification.py 


OUTPUT FILES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

search_log.csv        - Complete record of search queries and results
methodology_report.txt - Methods text suitable for publication
